# Step-by-step prompts, gates and examples

Use this file in Start mode. Each step has: what to ask, how to sharpen it, the gate, and good and bad examples.

## 1. Customer

**Ask:** "Who suffers most from this? Be as specific as you can: who they are, where they are, what situation they're in."

**Sharpen** by adding one of these filters each time: role, place, size, situation or trigger, current behaviour.
- "Restaurants" → "Halal restaurants in Kuantan" → "Halal restaurants and cafés in Kuantan that buy chicken from one supplier".

**Gate:** could the team list where to find 10 of these people this week? If not, narrow it, or pick a segment they can actually reach. Start from who they already know.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Students | First-year international students in campus dorms without a kitchen |
| SMEs | Frozen-food distributors in Pahang with 1–3 delivery vans |
| Everyone who eats | Office workers in KL who skip lunch on deadline days |

## 2. Problem

**Ask:** "What's hard for them? Write it the way *they* would complain about it."

**Sharpen:** add when it happens and what it costs (time, money, lost sales, stress, embarrassment).

**Gate:** you could ask "When did this last happen to you?" and expect a real story. It is the customer's pain, not the founder's goal, and it names no solution.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| They need an app for X | When my supplier runs out, I take chicken off the menu, because I can't trust a supplier I don't know |
| We can't get enough users | (the founder's problem, so rewrite it from the customer's side) |
| Logistics is inefficient | When a big order comes in suddenly, I can't find a lorry the same day, so I turn the order down |

## 3. Solution (parked)

If a solution comes up, write it on a grey brainstorm sticky marked "parked" and say in one line why: we haven't shown that this customer has this problem yet, so building anything now is a guess. It unlocks after a customer–problem experiment ends in **Persevere**.

## 4. Riskiest assumption (you draft it; team input is optional)

**Draft 5–8 assumptions** from the customer and problem. Write each as something the customer must *do, feel or have*:
- **Desirability:** they have the problem, often enough, painfully enough, and they're unhappy with their current workaround.
- **Viability:** they would spend time or money on a fix; someone pays.
- **Feasibility:** we can reach them; we can deliver; supply exists.

**Place each one on the workshop's assumption-ranking matrix (مصفوفة ترتيب الافتراضات):**

| الأهمية / الدليل (importance / evidence) | دليل قليل (little evidence) | دليل كثير (lots of evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **أهمية عالية** (high: if it's wrong, the idea falls) | المربع (1): **نختبره الآن** (square 1: test it now) | المربع (2): نراقبه (square 2: monitor it) |
| **أهمية منخفضة** (low) | المربع (3): نختبره لاحقًا (square 3: test it later) | المربع (4): لا يحتاج إلى اختبار الآن (square 4: no test needed now) |

Show a short table: assumption, square, and a reason. Recommend one assumption from square (1), with a one-line reason. On the board this is «الافتراض الذي لديه أقل قدر من البيانات، وهو جوهري لجدوى فرضيتي هو…» (the assumption with the least data, and core to my hypothesis, is…).

For a first experiment, the riskiest is usually desirability: "[customer] actually experiences [problem] at least [often] and does something about it today."

Then ask once: "Want to add your own, or go with this one?" Don't wait for every member to contribute.

**Gate:** exactly one assumption; it is behavioural and testable; it is the riskiest, not the easiest.

## 5. Method (طريقة الاختبار: «ما أرخص وأسرع طريقة لجمع دليل؟», the cheapest, fastest way to collect evidence)

Choose from the workshop's experiment ladder (سلّم التجارب), which runs from the cheapest to the strongest. The full table is in `glossary.md`.

| What the team knows | Rung on the ladder | Javelin video name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Little: the problem isn't confirmed | 4. المقابلات مع عملاء حقيقيين (interviews with real customers) | Interview | 5–10 face-to-face conversations about past behaviour |
| The problem is confirmed; the value of a solution is untested | 5. صفحة تسجيل أو طلب مسبق (sign-up page or pre-order) | Pre-sell | Show a mock-up and ask for an action: a sign-up, a letter of intent, or a pre-order paid on delivery (الدفع عند الاستلام). No money collected. |
| The value seems real; need to see it work | 6. نموذج أولي أو بيع تجريبي (prototype or trial sale) | Concierge | Deliver the service by hand to 2–5 customers and watch what they actually do |

Rungs 1–3 (AI simulation, desk research, a quick survey) are cheap preparation. They can sharpen questions but cannot decide a Javelin test on their own. In the workshop's online-test session, the guide's three tracks are:
- online research, recording each source's link and date
- a quick survey of «خمسة أسئلة على الأكثر عن السلوك الفعلي لا النوايا» (at most 5 questions about actual behaviour, not intentions)
- AI simulation for preparation

Recommend one method and say why. Prefer what the team can run this week with their own means: «أرخص طريقة لاختبار افتراضي هي…» (the cheapest way to test my assumption is…).

## 6. Success criterion

**Ask** in the board's own form: «سأجري التجربة مع # من العملاء، وأتوقع إشارة قوية من # منهم» (I will run the experiment with [#] customers and expect a strong signal from [#] of them). Add which behaviour counts, and by when.

**Check:**
- **Observable:** something you can count. "Describe a specific time in the last month", "sign up", "give contact details", "agree to a trial". Not "like it" or "say they'd use it".
- **Matches the method:** interviews count descriptions of the past; pre-sells count actions.
- **A real sample:** people truly in the segment. Under 5 is a signal only.
- **Meaningful:** if they hit it exactly, would they really invest more time? If not, raise it.

Don't suggest a number, not even "teams often pick half". **The team writes the numbers.** You only challenge them. Then lock it with today's date.

## 7. Team mode timings

Following the workshop's printed board («الزمن: 5 د»): customer 5 minutes, problem 5 minutes, solution 5 minutes (only after the problem is validated), assumptions 5 minutes. The original Javelin video allows 10 minutes for assumptions. Since you draft the assumptions here, 5 minutes for the team to react is enough. Collect stickies member by member, list them on the brainstorm side, and run a quick vote: each person picks one. Where the votes split, the facilitator decides. Keep the energy up and the steps short.
