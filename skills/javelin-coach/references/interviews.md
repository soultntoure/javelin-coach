# Interview kit

Build a kit from the team's **current board**: their customer, problem, riskiest assumption, method and criterion. Every question must trace back to something on the board. Write the kit in the team's language.

## 1. Who to ask

- **Screening questions (2–3)** that prove someone is in the segment *before* the interview counts. Example, for "halal restaurants relying on one chicken supplier": "Do you serve chicken? How many suppliers do you buy it from?"
- **Where to find them:** physical places (markets, shop rows, campus), online groups, associations. Start with **who the team already knows**: family businesses, friends' workplaces, past employers. Then ask each interviewee for a referral.
- **How many to contact:** the criterion's denominator, plus about 50% extra for no-shows.
- **Who doesn't count:** classmates and family, unless they truly are the segment. People who only said "sounds nice" in passing. AI simulations.
- **Who to talk to inside a business:** the person who actually feels the pain and makes the decision (the owner or the purchaser), not whoever answers the phone.
- **Order of visits:** the easiest-to-reach first, to practise. Then the most typical. Then one sceptic or edge case.

## 2. Opener (adapt to the language)

> "Hi, we're students doing an entrepreneurship training exercise. We're not selling anything. We're trying to understand how [customers like you] deal with [situation]. Could we ask you a few questions for 10 minutes?"

> «السلام عليكم، نحن طلاب في تدريب على ريادة الأعمال، ولا نبيع أي شيء. نحاول أن نفهم كيف يتعامل [أمثالكم] مع [الموقف]. هل تسمحون لنا ببعض الأسئلة لمدة 10 دقائق؟»

Then ask permission before taking any notes or photos.

## 3. Question order and generator

Ask in this order. Generate the actual questions from the board; the patterns below show how.

| # | Stage | Purpose | Pattern → example (halal restaurant board) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Context | Warm up; confirm they're in the segment | "Tell me about your [business/role]. How do you [get X] today?" → "How do you buy your chicken today?" |
| 2 | Last time | Get a real story, not an opinion | "Tell me about the last time [problem situation] happened." → "When did your supplier last run out? What happened that day?" |
| 3 | Response | Their current workaround | "What did you do about it?" → "Who did you call? Did you find chicken?" |
| 4 | Cost | How much the pain matters | "What did that cost you, in money, time, customers or stress?" → "Did you take dishes off the menu? For how long?" |
| 5 | Riskiest-assumption probe | Test the one assumption, through behaviour | Turn the assumption into a past-behaviour question. Assumption "they trust a new supplier we verify" → "Last time you tried a new supplier, how did you decide to trust them? What did you check?" |
| 6 | Show or ask (pre-sell and prototype methods only) | Watch behaviour, don't ask for opinions | "Here's a rough version. Try [task]." Watch silently. Then: "If this existed, would you want to be first on the list? Can I take a contact to follow up?" |
| 7 | Commitment | Separate polite interest from real interest | "Can we follow up in [week]?" / "Would you try a [trial/sign-up]?" (no money) |
| 8 | Referral | Grow the sample | "Who else has this problem that we should talk to?" |
| 9 | Thanks | — | Thank them; say again that it's a training exercise |

**Turning an assumption into a past-behaviour question:**
- "They will pay for X" → "What do you pay today to deal with this? When did you last spend money on it?"
- "They will switch from Y" → "Have you ever switched from Y? What made you switch, or stay?"
- "It happens often" → "How many times in the last month? When was the most recent?"
- "They can't find a fix" → "What have you tried? Why didn't it work?"

## 4. Rules while interviewing

- Ask about **the past and specifics**, never "would you…?", "do you think…?" or "wouldn't it be great if…?"
- Don't pitch until stage 6, and only if the method requires it. Pitching earlier turns everything they say afterwards into politeness.
- Listen far more than you talk. When they mention a pain, dig in: "Tell me more", "Why?", "What happened next?"
- Write down their **exact words** for the strongest moments. These become the problem sticky and the learning.
- One person asks and one takes notes, where possible.
- Compliments ("great idea!") are not data. Facts about the past and commitments are.

## 5. Pre-sell and concierge variants

- **Pre-sell (no money collected):** show the offer, with a price if relevant, and ask for a concrete action: a sign-up form, contact details for launch, a written letter of intent, or an order paid on delivery. Count the actions, not the "yes"es.
- **Concierge:** deliver the service by hand to a few customers (find the supplier yourselves, make the call, arrange the delivery). Record what they actually used, repeated, paid for on delivery, or recommended.

## 7. Optional: rehearse with AI (preparation only)

The workshop's third online track. Build 3 personas from the team's customer description, one of them sceptical, using the guide's prompts (adapt the brackets):

1. **بناء الشخصية (build the persona):** «أنت [الاسم]، عمرك [العمر]، [الدراسة أو العمل]، تعيش في [المكان]. [صف يومك وأهدافك وإحباطاتك من بطاقة شخصية العميل]. سأجري معك مقابلة عن [المجال]. أجب بأسلوب طبيعي ومن واقع حياتك، ولا تجاملني. وإذا لم تكن المشكلة مهمة لك فقل ذلك بوضوح، وإذا لم تعرف الإجابة فقل لا أعرف.» (You are [name], aged [age], [study or work], living in [place]. [Your day, goals and frustrations, from the customer persona card.] I'll interview you about [domain]. Answer naturally, from your own life, and don't flatter me. If the problem doesn't matter to you, say so clearly; if you don't know, say "I don't know".)
2. **المقابلة (the interview):** open questions such as «كيف يبدو يومك المعتاد؟»، «ما أصعب ما في [المشكلة]؟»، «كيف تحلها الآن؟»، «لماذا؟» (What does a normal day look like? What's hardest about [the problem]? How do you solve it now? Why?). Avoid «هل ستـ...؟» ("will you…?").
3. **العميل المتشكك (the sceptical customer):** «أنت الآن عميل متشكك جرّب حلولًا مشابهة وخاب أمله. اذكر بصراحة أهم ثلاثة أسباب قد تمنعك من استخدام هذا الحل: [وصف الحل في جملتين].» (You're now a sceptical customer who tried similar solutions and was disappointed. Honestly list the top three reasons you wouldn't use this: [the solution in two sentences].)
4. **التلخيص (the summary):** «لخّص المقابلات السابقة في: أكثر الآلام تكرارًا، وأقوى الاعتراضات، والمعلومات التي تحتاج إلى تحقق من عملاء حقيقيين.» (Summarise the interviews: the most repeated pains, the strongest objections, and what still needs checking with real customers.)

Label every simulated answer **"AI simulation: preparation, not evidence"**. Use it to sharpen the questions and to anticipate objections. Never add it to the tally, in the guide's words: «نستخدمه للاستعداد لا للإثبات» (we use it to prepare, not to prove). Never put real interviewees' personal data into the persona.
